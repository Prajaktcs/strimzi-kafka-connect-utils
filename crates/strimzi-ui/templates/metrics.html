Records/sec uses Kafka Connect's native rolling task rates, not counter differences. Connector rates are sums, never averages. Totals are current task-lifetime counters and can decrease after restarts or reassignment.

Source written means records written to Kafka. Sink read means records read from Kafka; sink sent means records passed to the sink plugin, not durable downstream commits.

Unsupported means no usable metric was exported. Unreachable means collection failed without a reading; stale values are historical. Ambiguous means duplicate task ownership or series, so no complete sum is shown. Missing task measurements or unhealthy workers also prevent complete connector sums.

Debezium streaming

Database connection and streaming measurements are shown per connector, task, worker, Debezium domain and server, without summing counters. Support varies by connector and version; unavailable or collection error readings are never zero. Stale readings are explicitly last observed, not current connection health.

Source delay is the last event's timestamp-to-processing delay in milliseconds, not Kafka consumer-group lag or downstream delivery. Clock skew affects it; negative delay is unavailable. During idle periods, the retained delay is not a wall-clock backlog estimate.

Streaming events and create/update/delete totals are cumulative task/session counters since restart or reset, not durable Kafka output. Snapshot-context counters are excluded.

Queues and backpressure

Queue utilization is derived per connector task as (total capacity − remaining capacity) ÷ total capacity; byte utilization divides current by maximum bytes. Both are shown per task, never averaged or summed across tasks, and no alert threshold is invented: utilization near 1.0 means the queue is filling, and an exhausted queue is a connector failure risk.

Missing utilization means zero or inconsistent capacity, not zero occupancy. Byte readings are shown exactly as the connector exports them — without max.queue.size.in.bytes the exporter may emit a real zero or nothing; only the derived byte utilization requires a positive maximum, so it alone turns unavailable rather than zero. Snapshot and streaming contexts report the same underlying queue in separate labeled rows.

Active records are records the Kafka Connect task holds in its pipeline without having fully committed them — source tasks poll the database before writing Kafka, sink tasks consume Kafka before the plugin is done. Active records are not Kafka consumer lag. The native Connect lag-max column reports the task's sink-record-lag-max, not the complete consumer-group committed-offset backlog shown above.
